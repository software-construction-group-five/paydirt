Content and balance

  • Agree on the final item catalog (SDD Contract 18)

    Decide which additional weapons, armor, and consumables belong in this increment, including unlock levels, damage dice, defense, healing, and any on-hit effects. The current nine-item catalog contains Pickaxe, Health Potion, Shovel, Lamp Fuel, and five helmets: Rusty, Lead, Iron, Steel, and Pete’s Helmet. Helmet bonuses increase from +1 to +5, unlocking at depths 0, 3, 6, 9, and 12. Helmets occupy 5% of floor loot and successful equipment drops; only the best unlocked helmet tier spawns. Weapons roll depth-scaled bonuses and have a 1% chance of bleed, weakness, or lightning; damage labels show the effect color. Record whether additional consumable behaviors are required; the existing potion behavior is already implemented.

Completed work

These capabilities are implemented together on feature/new-items and have automated coverage. Completion here describes code behavior; manual acceptance and release review are tracked above.

  • Turn counter, lamp, and fuel supplies

    Player Stats tracks elapsed spent turns and 200 starting lamp fuel. Sight decreases linearly from 10 to 5 at 50 fuel, then to 1 at zero. Every spent action burns fuel and refreshes sight; menus remain free. Lamp Fuel refills via inventory use, shares consumable capacity, and leaves 199 after its action. Exactly one extra supply spawns on the starting floor; descent adds one only below 150 fuel before that turn. Fuel is excluded from random loot and drops. Counter and fuel persist on descent and reset on restart.

  • Player sessions, movement, and doors (SDD Contracts 1–3, 6–9, 21)

    New Game creates the player, floor, starting equipment, and log. Cardinal movement checks terrain and occupancy; bumping a closed door opens it without moving, and bumping an enemy attacks without moving. Movement and door opening refresh sight. Pause and return-to-menu navigation are integrated.

  • Generated floors, entities, and visibility (SDD Contracts 8–12, 18–20)

    Connected floors contain rooms, corridors, doors, rocks, water, and stairs. Depth-based populations exclude the entire starting room and use distinct initial spawn tiles. Level owns stable enemy IDs and original ground items. Terrain walkability and actor occupancy are separate. Line of sight, persistent exploration memory, and themed rendering hide entities outside current sight. Seeded tests cover generation, placement, connectivity, and depth limits.

  • Enemy detection, movement, and attacks (SDD Contracts 9, 10, 13, 14)

    Slimes, Ghosts, and Goblins use species detection ranges with terrain line of sight. Detected enemies approach or flee based on health; blocked paths wait or try another improving cardinal step. Attacks require cardinal adjacency. Game revalidates intents and schedules one decision per surviving enemy.

  • Dice combat, effects, drops, and turn scheduling (SDD Contracts 2, 7, 10, 13, 14)

    Player and enemy attacks roll damage dice, apply defense with minimum one damage, and log actual HP lost. New games equip a 2d4 Pickaxe. Weapon-effect support creates independent durations; effects tick and expire through the shared turn boundary. Direct and effect kills share immediate removal and once-only equipment drops. Player death stops processing and stores the cause and final depth.

  • Inventory, equipment, and potion use (SDD Contracts 7, 11, 15–18, 20, 21)

    Carried copies and equipped slots are selectable. Pickup, drop, swap, unequip, and use preserve original item data and enforce category capacity. Armor affects effective defense. Health Potions restore up to 12 HP, remain carried at full health, and consume one selected copy on successful healing. Attempts reuse the combat turn boundary, including failures and death while inventory is open.

  • Descent, game-over history, and restart (SDD Contracts 1, 4, 5, 7, 19–21)

    Explicit stair use checks position and depth overflow, ticks player effects before replacement, and preserves surviving player health, effects, equipment, and inventory. Lethal transition effects retain the old floor and final depth. GameOver scrolls the full multiline history; short terminals prioritize messages. Returning to menu and restarting clear session state.

  • Configurable presentation and controls (SDD Contracts 20, 21)

    The launcher loads theme and keybindings before terminal setup using project-relative paths. Rendering consumes borrowed views without mutating gameplay. The ? controls overlay shows the underlying screen’s configured bindings and restores its selections and history position without spending turns. Automated checks cover custom glyphs, visibility, remapped keys, and small/resized layouts.

Implemented rules to preserve

  • Moves, waits, bump attacks, door opening, and inventory attempts spend one turn, including rejected attempts. Menus, selection changes, pause, help, redraws, and history scrolling spend none.
  • Normal turn order is player action → surviving enemy actions → player effects → surviving enemy effects. New weapon effects tick that turn. Lethal player damage stops remaining processing.
  • Accepted descent ticks only player effects on the old floor before replacement. Neither floor runs enemy actions or enemy effects during the transition. Failed descent spends an ordinary turn.
  • Only pressing > on stairs descends; walking onto them does not. Dead players cannot perform gameplay actions.
  • Game owns the player, RNG, navigation, and turn scheduling. Level owns terrain, enemies, and ground items. AI proposes intents; the TUI translates input and renders views.
  • Enemy IDs are stable within one floor and discarded when it is replaced. Ground-item indices shift after removal. Item transfers preserve the owned copy, including dice and effects.

See README: combat turn order and level entity integration notes for the concrete interfaces and timing rules.
